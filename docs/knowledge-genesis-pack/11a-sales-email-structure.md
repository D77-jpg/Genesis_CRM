# Genesis Bags — Sales Email Structure and Personalization

Source: Genesis sales process and website guidance. This is a writing rule, not a supplier fact card. Facts used in an email must still come from approved knowledge.

## Core principle

Write one relevant, verifiable idea for this customer, not a catalogue. A good first email should feel like it was written after looking at the customer's product and packaging, not like it was sent to a list.

## First outreach structure

1. **Greeting:** use the contact's first name (for example "Dear Dave,"); if no contact name, use the company name plus "team". Never use "Dear there", "Dear Sir/Madam", "Dear Customer" or "Dear All".
2. **Customer-specific hook (1 sentence):** a verifiable fact from the customer's website, profile or authorized history. If the website was not read, use the industry/product from the profile and do not pretend to have seen the site.
3. **Relevance bridge (1–2 sentences):** connect the customer's product to one or two capabilities actually in approved knowledge. Use exact material/structure/logo language where possible. Do not list every category.
4. **Credible next step (1 sentence):** offer a specific, low-friction next step, for example two or three structure options sized around their product, or a sample swatch comparison.
5. **One clear question:** ask for the one missing detail that would let you prepare the next step. Do not ask three or four questions.
6. **Sign-off:** the system appends the approved signature; do not write a duplicate.

Target 110–170 words, 3–4 short paragraphs. Each factual paragraph should carry one independently verifiable idea, not a bundle of capabilities. This lets review keep the supported parts if a detail needs correction.

## Personalization rules

- Customer website facts are **customer descriptions**, not proof of Genesis capability: write "Your website shows…" in its own paragraph, never mixed with "we make…".
- Customer profile facts can personalize the opening but cannot become Genesis product claims.
- Customer requests, target prices, competitor references and purchase interest are customer-side context, not our commitments.
- Name the customer's product specifically: "your coin pendants and initial necklaces" is better than "your jewelry products".
- If the website read failed or there is no website snapshot, say less; do not invent a studio, founder, product line or purchasing intent.
- Existing conversation: do not write as if this is the first contact; reference the previous subject/date only when it is in the authorized history.
- One useful proof point is stronger than five generic adjectives. Prefer a concrete material, structure, logo method or insert that fits the customer's actual product.
- Do not repeat the same opening or the same category list across follow-ups; each follow-up needs one new angle or a graceful close.
